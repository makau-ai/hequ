claude-opus-4-6 APPROVE

The coupling between Fick's law of diffusion and Fourier's law of heat conduction is a textbook example of structural analogy between linear transport equations: J = -D·(dC/dx) maps to q = -k·(dT/dx) under a consistent variable renaming. The concentration gradient and temperature gradient play identical structural roles (driving forces in their respective flux laws), and the tier-2 similarity classification with an identity transfer function accurately reflects this isomorphism rather than claiming the two quantities are literally identical. The Onsager reciprocity check correctly notes that a 1×1 coupling matrix is trivially symmetric, and the other constraint checks are properly marked not applicable. The Buckingham π groups are dimensionally consistent within each equation. No spurious emergent properties are claimed. This is a well-established and physically meaningful cross-domain analogy in irreversible thermodynamics.

openai-gpt-5 REJECT

Despite the structural similarity of Fick’s and Fourier’s laws, dC/dx (ConcentrationGradient) and dT/dx (TemperatureGradient) are different physical quantities with incompatible dimensions; an identity transfer function is therefore not physically plausible and is not supported by the semantic descriptors. A meaningful cross-domain link would require a named transform (e.g., nondimensionalization or phenomenological cross-coefficients such as Soret/Dufour relating appropriate forces/fluxes), not direct equality of the gradients. The physical-constraint filter’s Onsager “pass” concerns symmetry of transport matrices and does not validate variable identity, and the reported π-groups are trivial restatements of the base laws rather than genuine emergent properties.

gemini-2.5-pro REJECT

The proposed coupling incorrectly equates a concentration gradient with a temperature gradient via an identity transfer function. While Fick's Law and Fourier's Law are structurally analogous and describe phenomena that are physically coupled in nature (e.g., thermodiffusion), a concentration gradient is not a temperature gradient. They are distinct physical quantities with different dimensions. This proposal confuses a valid mathematical analogy with an invalid physical identity, and as a result, no genuine emergent properties are discovered.
